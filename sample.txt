The Mint crew: the Professor (mastermind, outside), Tokyo (narrator, impulsive), Berlin (second-in-command, ruthless), Nairobi (counterfeiting expert), Rio (young hacker), Denver (muscle, cheerful), Moscow (Denver’s father, tunnel digger), Oslo (silent enforcer) and Helsinki (Oslo’s cousin, ex-soldier).

Added for the Bank of Spain: Palermo (brilliant planner and engineer), Bogotá (gold-melting expert), Marseille, Manila, Stockholm, and others.

The opposition: Raquel Murillo (Lisbon), the negotiator who later joins the Professor; Alicia Sierra, the ruthless inspector; Colonel Tamayo, the brutal intelligence officer; and Arturo, the Mint’s defiant director.

The Planning: The Professor spent years designing his plan. Its core idea was that the crew would not steal existing money, which would be traced and hunted. They would lock themselves in the Royal Mint of Spain and print their own, about 2.4 billion euros. Printed money was not stolen, so there was no victim and no serial numbers on file. He recruited eight criminals with nothing to lose and trained them for five months on an isolated farm. Each took the name of a city so nobody would know anyone’s real identity. The rules were strict: no personal relationships, no real names, no violence, and never leave without the plan.

The Entry and the Hostages: On the day of the heist, the crew entered the Royal Mint in red jumpsuits and Dalí masks and took 67 hostages, including a school group. The Professor’s first masterstroke was to dress the hostages in the same jumpsuits and masks. The police outside could no longer tell hostages from robbers, which made a direct assault dangerous and gave the Professor leverage.

The Cat-and-Mouse Game: From a hidden hideout, the Professor negotiated through Inspector Raquel Murillo, who was assigned to the case. The two began a mental duel. Raquel was brilliant and slowly worked out how he thought, and, without knowing who he was, she began falling for him. Inside the Mint, Nairobi ran the printing presses day and night, while Moscow dug a tunnel under the building to carry the money out.

Cracks in the Plan: Things quickly went off-script. Berlin broke the no-violence rule and treated the hostages with cruelty, creating tension with the Professor. Tokyo and Rio’s secret relationship caused emotional disorder, against the Professor’s rules. Arturo, the Mint’s director, kept trying to sabotage the crew. Oslo was seriously injured, and the tunnel ran into trouble. Raquel kept uncovering clues, and the police closed in on the Professor’s hideout. The Professor had to improvise constantly, using decoys and misdirection to stay ahead.

The Climax and Escape: After about eleven days, the police stormed the Mint. Berlin stayed behind to hold them off, a final stand that bought the others time. The crew escaped through the tunnel with about 984 million euros. The 2.4 billion target was not reached because the heist was cut short. The police were left realizing they had been outplayed from the start.

Five Years Later: The crew scattered across the world and lived quietly on their millions. Tokyo and Rio settled on a remote island, and the Professor lived in hiding in Palawan, Philippines, with Raquel, who had by then joined his side. But Tokyo’s recklessness drew attention. Rio was captured by a special police unit led by Colonel Tamayo and brutally interrogated. Tokyo called the Professor, who faced a choice with no clean way out. He decided to do what he had sworn never to do again: reassemble the crew for a second heist.

The New Plan: The goal was twofold: steal a vast fortune in gold from the Bank of Spain, and force the government to give Rio back. The Professor recruited new members (Palermo, Bogotá, Marseille, Manila, Stockholm and others) and again named them after cities. The plan was bigger than the Mint. They would take over the Bank, hold hundreds of hostages, and melt the gold into liquid so it could be moved out without being carried through the front door. Once again they entered in red jumpsuits and Dalí masks.

A Far More Dangerous Enemy: Alicia Sierra, a pregnant and ruthless inspector, became the Professor’s most cunning opponent. She went after his weak points: his family, his love for Raquel, and the secrets he thought were buried. Colonel Tamayo, who still held Rio, used every dirty trick against the crew. Inside the Bank, old and new members clashed, hostages fought back, and Berlin’s son, Rafael, arrived with a grudge and a hidden agenda that threatened everything. Plan after plan cracked, and the crew paid in blood. Moscow, the old miner from the first heist, was killed, a loss that devastated his son Denver and the whole gang.

The Turning Point: The Professor kept going by improvising, using misdirection and increasingly desperate gambles to stay ahead of Sierra and Tamayo. He eventually got Rio back, but the cost showed that this was no longer a clean game of strategy. It had become a war, and the story pushed on toward its final stand.
